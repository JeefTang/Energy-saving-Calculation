Biogas to power

From the pond to the busbar

A POME biogas power plant is a chain, not a machine. Gas quality at the engine inlet decides whether you get 8,000 hours or 4,000 hours between overhauls. We design the whole chain and take responsibility for the interface.

Process chain

Six stages, one gas specification

CaptureCovered lagoon or CSTR; HDPE/geomembrane cover, condensate traps, gas collection headers.
BufferGas holder or membrane storage to absorb mill load swings and protect the engine.
DesulfuriseBiological scrubber for bulk H₂S, iron-oxide or activated carbon polishing.
ConditionChilling and coalescing to remove water and siloxane carry-over; blower to engine pressure.
GenerateGas engine + alternator, island or parallel; flared safely during trips.
Recover heatJacket water ~90 °C and exhaust to hot water, boiler feedwater or steam.

Gas specification

What the engine will accept

These limits are the basis of our warranty discussions with the engine supplier. If your gas cannot meet them, the treatment scope changes — we would rather say so before the order than after.

Methane content≥ 50% (design 55–65%)
H₂S at engine inlet≤ 200 ppm, target ≤ 100 ppm
Water dew point≤ 10 °C above ambient at pressure
Gas temperature≤ 40 °C
Inlet pressure5–15 kPa, stable within ±1 kPa
Particulates / oilEssentially free; coalescing filter fitted
Pressure fluctuation≤ 10% per minute

The most common mistake: accepting raw POME gas at 2,000–4,000 ppm H₂S. It halves the lube-oil life, doubles the maintenance cost, and the saving is invisible until the first overhaul. Desulfurisation is not optional.

Commercial models

Three ways to structure the project

Model 1

Equipment supply

Genset, gas train, control panel, heat recovery. Client or their contractor handles civils and balance of plant. Lowest entry cost, most interfaces on the client side.

Model 2

EPC turnkey

Single contract from pond cover to grid-tie, with local civil and mechanical works executed by KSTS under CIDB registration and commissioning witnessed by DOSH.

Model 3

BOO / shared savings

We fund and own the plant, the mill buys power or gas at a discount to its current cost. No capital outlay for the mill; the return comes from the spread over the contract term.

Before we quote

Seven pieces of data we need

With these we can produce a defensible capacity and budget. Without them, any number is a guess with a decimal point.

  • FFB throughput, t/h and operating days per year
  • POME pond: number of stages, total volume, HRT, whether covered
  • Measured biogas flow and composition (CH₄, CO₂, H₂S, temperature)
  • Electricity tariff or diesel consumption and price
  • Grid connection status and transformer capacity
  • Boiler fuel, steam demand and pressure, if heat or biomethane is the target
  • Load profile: base load, peaks, night shift

Send the data, get a capacity

We reply with a gas balance, a recommended unit size and an indicative budget — normally within three working days.
