Biomethane & bio-CNG

Biogas upgraded to boiler-grade gas

If the mill’s boiler runs on diesel, LPG or natural gas, the fastest payback is often not electricity — it is replacing that fuel with biomethane at ≥97% CH₄, produced on site from the same POME.

Process

From 60% to 97% in five steps

DesulfuriseBiological plus iron oxide to protect the upgrading media and the compressor.
CompressTo the pressure required by the selected separation technology.
DryRefrigerated dryer and coalescing filters; no free water into the sorbent.
Separate CO₂PSA, water scrubbing or membrane — selected on gas volume and site constraints.
DeliverOdorise and send to the burner, or compress to 250 bar for tube-trailer bio-CNG.

Technology selection

PSA, water scrubbing or membrane

There is no universal best choice. It depends on gas volume, required purity, power cost and how much space you have next to the mill.

Gas quality we guarantee

  • CH₄ ≥ 97% (adjustable to 96–99%)
  • CO₂ ≤ 2–3%
  • H₂S ≤ 5 ppm after polishing
  • Water dew point ≤ −40 °C at pressure
  • O₂ ≤ 0.5%
  • Calorific value compatible with the existing burner after nozzle/jet change

Burner retrofit is part of the scope. A biomethane burner needs a different gas train, orifice and flame-detection setup from diesel or LPG. We coordinate that with the boiler supplier rather than leaving it to the mill.

Bio-CNG

When there is no pipeline, build a virtual one

Compressed biomethane at 250 bar in tube trailers moves gas to estates, factories or refuelling points without a pipeline. For remote Sabah and Sarawak sites this is often the only practical route to market.

Step 1

Compression

Multi-stage compression from upgrading pressure to 250 bar, with inter-stage drying and gas cooling.

Step 2

Storage & loading

Stationary cascade storage plus a loading arm for tube trailers; metering and custody-transfer records.

Step 3

Off-site use

Pressure reduction skid at the receiving site, or a daughter-station arrangement for fleet refuelling.
